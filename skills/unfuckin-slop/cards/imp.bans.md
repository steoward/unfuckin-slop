# Absolute ban

- **A kicker or eyebrow above a heading.** No brief earns it back. The heading carries its own weight; delete the label and let the heading speak.

The only ban with no exceptions. Everything below is a category default: the brief's own words can earn it, but reaching for one when the axis is free means you were not deciding.

# Refused defaults

**Page scaffolds**

- Same-size cards of icon + heading + text as the page structure. Cards are the lazy container; nested cards are always wrong.
- The hero-metric template: big number, small label, supporting stats, accent.
- Section numbers (01 / 02 / 03) unless the sequence itself carries information.
- A modal for a task that needs neither interruption nor protected focus.

**Surface habits**

- Gradient text. Emphasis comes from weight or size.
- Glass and blur as decoration rather than a specific material effect.
- A coloured `border-left`/`border-right` above 1px on cards, list items, callouts, or alerts.
- Hard offset shadows (`4px 4px 0`) outside a world that is genuinely neobrutalist.
- Sparklines, progress rings, and soft rounded rectangles standing in for content.
- Monospace as a costume for "technical" rather than for code, data, or measurement.
- A system display face (Impact, Arial Black, the platform sans) as the display voice of an own-world page. Source a face whose character matches the approved lettering; the closest installed font is a failure, not a fallback.
- Unicode glyphs or emoji as the icon system. Icons come from a real library or authored SVG, in one consistent stroke and weight.
- Geometric masks standing in for organic contours. A circle or radial-gradient cutout approximating a photographic edge reads worse than omitting the effect.
- Light or dark picked by category. Pick it from the use scene: who, where, under what ambient light.

# How to refuse

Rewrite the element with a different structure. Do not soften the banned pattern: a 2px side stripe is still a side stripe.
